The pitch

Fitness apps record what you did. AZfit works out what you should do next. Every set, meal and scan becomes data that an Opus-class agent can reason over, and it uses tools to adjust your training and diet the way a good coach would. It runs on the Claude subscription you already pay for, through a small free bridge on your Mac.

Pillar 1 · Train

Every exercise is tagged with the muscles it works and how much fatigue it costs.

The exercise library ships complete, so you can build workouts by hand or ask the agent for them. Each exercise records its primary and secondary muscles with involvement weights, movement pattern, equipment, joint stress, and a fatigue cost. That tagging lets one logged set of RDLs update your hamstrings, glutes, lower back and grip at once.

Logging that captures effort

Weight, reps and reps in reserve on every set, with one-tap RPE if you prefer it. RIR is the most important input in the app: it is how the agent separates a strength drop from tiredness.

Recovery

Readiness per muscle

Fatigue decays at a rate set for each muscle, then gets adjusted by sleep and soreness check-ins. Legs can be at 40% while arms are fresh.

Detraining

Skips are modeled

Missed days are logged as gaps. After about two weeks off, the next session starts lighter and builds back up over several sessions. Nobody gets lectured.

Overreaching

Catches burnout early

The warning sign is e1RM falling at the same RIR over two or more sessions, plus poor sleep and high soreness. The response is a deload or a volume cut.

Pillar 2 · Eat

Calories are a ruler. It only has to be the same ruler every day.

Getting the absolute number right is close to impossible. Labels can be off by 20%, photos misjudge oil, and portions drift. That is fine. If you gain weight eating "2,000", the agent lowers your target to 1,700 and watches. Your logged number becomes accurate for you through the scale's feedback. Consistency matters more than accuracy, so AZfit puts its effort into logging the same meal the same way each time.

Protein is the daily target

Calories get tuned once a week. Protein is the number to hit every day, because it decides how much muscle you keep on a cut and build on a recomp. The target is set per kg of body weight. If body fat is high, it uses goal weight instead, so the number doesn't get inflated.

Cut2.0 – 2.2 g/kg
Recomp1.8 – 2.2 g/kg
Build1.6 – 2.0 g/kg
Per meal~0.4 g/kg · 3 to 5 meals
When calories go down, protein stays and carbs and fat take the cut. If you miss protein three or more days a week, the agent suggests easy swaps from foods you already log instead of a bigger number. Onboarding asks about kidney disease. If you have it, your doctor sets the protein number and the agent won't change it.

Only complete days count

The calibration loop assumes your log is the whole day. A day with only lunch logged would make maintenance look hundreds of calories too low. So every day is marked complete or partial, and only complete days go into the maintenance math.

How it's marked"Done for today" tap, or asked next morning

The agent asks about a suspected partial day before it excludes it. It never assumes. With fewer than 10 complete days, the Sunday review holds calories and says why.
When the target moves

Weigh in daily, decide weekly, and read the scale next to the tape and the bar.

Daily weight swings 1 to 2 kg with water, salt, carbs and digestion, so a single weigh-in is mostly noise. A plain 7-day average is better, but one salty weekend still moves it and it lags by half a week. AZfit keeps a smoothed trend weight, where each new weigh-in counts for about 10%, and only looks at its slope over the last two to three weeks. The scale also can't tell fat from muscle or water, so before changing calories the agent checks it against your waist measurement and your strength.

The decision rules

1Weigh daily, decide weekly. The calorie target is only reviewed on Sunday, never because of one weigh-in.
2Read the slope. Implied maintenance = average intake on complete days − (trend change in kg × 7,700) ÷ days, over a 14 to 21 day window.
3Two weeks off target before moving. One off week is marked "watching". The exception is losing faster than 1% of body weight a week.
4Small steps. 100 to 200 per change, at most one change every two weeks, so the effect of each change can be seen.
5Flag known water events. Starting creatine, the first two weeks of a new program, travel, illness, or the menstrual cycle (compared phase to phase) extend the window instead of triggering a change.

Reading the signals together

Muscle grows at most around 0.25 to 1 kg a month, so a 1.5 kg jump in a week is water. Waist and e1RM settle what the scale can't.

Pillar 3 · Measure

Prop the phone up, turn in place, and get a body scan.

Lean the phone against the bathroom wall and step back about two meters. Voice cues walk you through a slow turn. On Pro models the rear LiDAR captures depth from every angle, since the front TrueDepth camera can't reach that far. Other iPhones can't measure depth well enough for trustworthy numbers, so there the scan becomes a 3D progress model: you can turn it around, put two dates side by side, or slide between them to see how you've changed. Measurements and a body fat range come only from LiDAR. As with calories, the trend matters more than any single number, so it asks for the same spot, the same light, first thing in the morning.

It captures one continuous turn, then keeps four key frames (front, left, back, right). The full turn gives the mesh fit more data than four still photos would.

Every iPhone, not just Pro

LiDAR gives numbers. Every iPhone gives a progress model.

Only Pro and Pro Max iPhones have LiDAR. Photogrammetry works on the rest for food, because a plate holds still. It works poorly for a person turning in place, because the body shifts between frames, and a body fitted to silhouettes is too rough to report body fat or circumferences honestly. So base iPhones show change visually and leave the numbers to a tape measure, a 30-second waist check the agent asks for weekly. The same spot, light and time of day matter more than the device.

Pro · Pro Max

Measured depth

  • Body: LiDAR depth at about 2 m from every angle of the turn, fused into one point cloud and fitted to a body model. Tightest circumference ranges.
  • Food: one shot gives a real-scale depth map of the plate. Volume is the space between the food's surface and the table.
Base iPhones

Progress model

  • Body: Apple's Vision framework finds your silhouette and pose in each frame of the turn, and a body model is fitted to them for scale and shape. You get a 3D progress model and four matched photos per date. No body fat number. Waist comes from the tape.
  • Food: a 3-second sweep around the plate. ARKit tracks the camera at real-world scale using the motion sensors and finds the table, frames are triangulated into a height map above the table, and volume comes from that. A single photo falls back to your plate's diameter, measured once, as the ruler.

Volume is not calories. Density, oil and hidden sauce still come from the description, so the kitchen scale stays the most accurate rung on the ladder on every phone. Scans and 3D models stay on your devices. The agent only ever sees the numbers, never the images.

Pillar 4 · Apple Health

Apple Health fills in the hours you aren't logging.

Most of what decides your recovery and your maintenance calories happens outside the gym: steps, sleep, other sports. AZfit reads these from Apple Health with background delivery, so new samples arrive without opening the app, and syncs them to the bridge. The agent sees them as context for decisions, never as a second calorie budget.

What it reads, and what it does with it

Exercise calories are never added back. Watch estimates for active energy are often far off, and the calibration loop already measures what you actually burn. Activity only explains why maintenance moved.
Consent is separate. Each Health category can be switched on or off. Health data only reaches Claude when the agent asks for it through a tool, and only after you agree on its own screen, because Apple requires explicit consent before health data goes to any third party, your own Claude account included.
The coach

The agent has every tool a human coach would, with limits on what it changes alone.

Claude reads your logs through structured tools and makes changes through other tools. Routine progression, like +2.5 kg after you hit the top of a rep range, applies automatically. Structural changes go to you as a plan to approve: swapping an exercise, adding volume to a muscle, changing calories by 150 or more, scheduling a deload. Every change records its reason, so you can always ask why.

Walkthrough · your first week

From install to the first plan change.

Day 0 · 5 min

Pair the bridge

Install the bridge on your Mac. It shows a QR code, you scan it with the phone, and that's the pairing done. The bridge checks that claude is signed in. Then you set a goal (lose fat, recomp, or build), your schedule, your equipment, and any injuries, and choose which Apple Health categories AZfit can read.

Day 0

Baseline scan and weigh-in

The first scan sets your starting measurements. The agent drafts a four-week block for your schedule and shows which muscles each day trains, so you can see the split before agreeing to it.

Day 1

First workout

Today's session opens with the warm-up sets already calculated. Log each set with weight, reps and RIR, and a rest timer starts. When you finish, the heat map updates and the agent gives a one-line note: "Bench felt easier than prescribed, next week's load goes up."

Day 1 to 7

Log food at whatever detail you have time for

Snap a photo and it's logged. Add a few words ("chicken shawarma plate, half the rice") or the plate weight when you can. Meals you eat often become one-tap favorites that log the same number every time, which is what the calibration loop needs. A protein ring fills through the day. At night, one tap marks the day complete.

Day 3

Life happens

You skip leg day. Nothing gets reset or rescheduled into a pile. The agent moves the essential lower-body work into Friday's session and drops the accessories.

Day 7

Sunday review

Claude reads the week: sessions done, effort per set, volume per muscle, weight trend against intake, sleep. You get a short summary and a list of proposed changes, with reasons, to approve or skip.

Week 3+

Calibration

Once there are about two weeks of weigh-ins and at least 10 complete days, AZfit replaces the formula's estimate with your measured maintenance calories, in your own logging units. From then on, diet changes come from your data instead of averages.

Product opinions

What we chose, and what we left out.

RIR over RPE

"2 reps left" is easier to judge than "RPE 8", and it feeds the e1RM math directly. RPE is still accepted and converted.

Trends over snapshots

Weight, scans and calories are always shown smoothed, with a range. Calorie targets move only at the weekly review. No single weigh-in should set the mood for your day.

Proposals, not surprises

The agent changes the plan through a visible diff. Only routine progression applies without asking.

Your data stays local

Your data lives on your phone and your Mac. There's no AZfit cloud, so there are no AZfit accounts to breach. The only data that leaves is what the agent reads through Claude on your own account.

No streak shame

No guilt notifications or broken-streak badges. A skipped workout is just data the agent plans around.

No social feed

AZfit is a coach, not a network. Shareable progress cards can come later.

Next phase

Design the data model so the agent has full context: every exercise tag, set, meal estimate with its confidence, whether each day was complete, scan, and plan change with its reason. The goal is that no recommendation depends on information the agent cannot see.
